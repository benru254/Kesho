# Kesho — the psychology behind the app

Kesho is built for one specific person: someone who is **often lazy, often led by feelings, and sometimes
capable of extraordinary effort**. The productive days look after themselves. Every feature below exists
for the other days.

The rule over all of them: **honest, never cruel.** Shame makes people quit. Kesho never shows a broken
streak, a red failure screen or a guilt message. It tells the truth with numbers and always leaves a door open.

---

## 1. Your own words beat any motivational quote — *My why*
**Feature:** first-run questions (why · who you are becoming · what pulls you off track · your plan).
Your answers come back to you in the Pause button, in the focus timer, in "welcome back" messages and on
the Today screen.

**Why:** a reason you wrote yourself is far harder to ignore than a stranger's slogan. On weak days you forget
why you started; Kesho remembers it for you, in your own voice.

## 2. Identity before habits — *"I am becoming…"*
**Feature:** the identity sentence is shown when you complete a full day, and the day counter says **DAY N**.

**Why:** people act in line with who they believe they are. Every check-in is a small vote for
"I am someone who shows up". The day number counts days you showed up — it **never resets**, so a gap never
erases the evidence you have already built.

## 3. Decide in advance — *If-then plans and Tomorrow's ONE thing*
**Feature:** "When that happens, I will…" (shown by the Pause button). The night check-in asks for
**tomorrow's one thing**, and the morning reminder names it.

**Why:** a decision made calmly the night before needs no willpower in the morning. "When X, I will Y"
plans are one of the best-studied tools for closing the gap between intention and action.

## 4. One thing, not ten — *THE ONE THING (gold task)*
**Feature:** max 3 tasks; one of them is gold. Finishing it says "the day is already won".

**Why:** long to-do lists create the feeling of failure even on good days. One clear priority makes
starting easy and finishing likely.

## 5. Ride the wave — *The Pause button* (⏸ in the middle of the tab bar)
**Feature:** name what is pulling you → breathe for 60 seconds (in 4s, out 6s) → read your why → check
how strong the pull is → if still strong, your plan or 15 focused minutes instead → log honestly
(held / slipped).

**Why:** urges rise, peak and fall like a wave, usually within minutes. Putting 60 seconds between the
feeling and the action is often enough for the decision to change. Slow out-breaths calm the body.
Logging "slipped" without shame keeps the data honest — and noticing is the actual skill.

## 6. Start smaller than you think — *Focus timer*
**Feature:** ▶ Focus on any task → 15 / 25 / 45 minutes → screen stays on, only the task and your why
are shown → "Did you finish it?"

**Why:** the hardest part of work is starting. A short, clearly-ended block lowers the cost of starting.
Focused minutes are counted for life, because focused hours are what income is made of.

## 7. Let the want cool down — *24-hour wait list* (Money tab)
**Feature:** park a want for 24 hours. Then "let it go" (the money is counted as **kept**) or "buy it on
purpose" (recorded under Wants).

**Why:** many purchases are moods, not decisions. Waiting a day lets the mood pass. If you still want it,
buying it is a real choice — no guilt.

## 8. Make progress visible — *The living sky*
**Feature:** the Today header follows the real time of day. The sun **rises as you complete the day**.
When you close the day, night falls and one star appears for every day you have shown up.

**Why:** people work harder as they see themselves getting closer to a goal. A sun that physically rises is
a progress bar you *feel*. On low-mood days the target drops (one task + close the day = full sun),
so the bar is reachable even then.

## 9. Celebrate real wins, in varied ways — *Celebrations & milestones*
**Feature:** small wins get a short toast, a vibration and a little confetti, with varying words. Milestones
(Day 7, 30, 100… · urges held · focus hours · KES saved · money kept) get a full-screen moment, once.

**Why:** a reward right after an action strengthens it; varied rewards keep feeling fresh. But if everything
is celebrated, nothing is — so only real wins count, and old data never causes a flood of celebrations.

## 10. Never miss twice — *No-guilt welcome back*
**Feature:** missing one day gives a gentle "never miss twice". Longer absences get "welcome back", sometimes
quoting your why, and reminders thin out instead of piling up.

**Why:** missing a single day barely matters to a habit. What breaks habits is the shame spiral after a
miss. Coming back is the real skill, and Kesho rewards it.

## 11. Fresh starts — *Mondays and the 1st of the month*
**Feature:** "New week. Clean slate." reminders.

**Why:** people are more willing to start over at natural new beginnings. Kesho uses those moments.

## 12. Honest mirror — *Weekly report*
**Feature:** each Sunday: showing up, sleep, movement, mood, stress, tasks, focus, urges held, money — vs last
week; patterns over 28 days (only with enough data, labelled "not proof"); **one** focus for next week.

**Why:** you can't change what you don't see. One focus gives you a direction; ten suggestions give you
nothing.

---

## What Kesho will not do
- No streak that breaks, no red "you failed", no guilt messages.
- No more than 3 daily reminders, and fewer when you are away.
- No fake insights: patterns only with enough data.
- No endless scrolling: the goal is ~2 minutes a day in the app and more life outside it.
- Kesho is a tool, not a therapist. If low mood lasts for weeks, talk to someone you trust or a professional.
